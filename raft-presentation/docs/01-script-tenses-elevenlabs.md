**Settings.** Stability 50–55 %, Similarity 75 %, Style exaggeration 15–20 %, Speaker boost on. Use the per-slide speed below (0.92–1.00).

**Workflow.**

1. Paste one slide's ElevenLabs-ready block at a time, generate, and listen for the tense verbs: they must be clear.
2. Download each take as `slide-01.mp3` … `slide-10.mp3`.
3. In Canva, drop each MP3 on its page and set the page duration to the audio length (or use Auto-play in the 3D scene, which is timed to the same script).

**Pronunciation already handled in the script.** Numbers are written as words (*four thousand five hundred square meters*), *R.C.* keeps its periods so it is read as letters, and *-ed* is written *E-D* so the voice does not read it as a word.

**Practice marks** in the scripts: `|` short pause (about 0.6 s) · `‖` long pause (about 1.2 s) · **bold** = the target-tense verb to stress.

## Slide 1: One Raft, One Continuous Slab

**Tense focus:** Present Simple · **Stage:** Project overview and CAD layout · **Speaking time:** ≈ 85 s (161 words)

### (a) Tense explanation

**Present Simple.** We describe permanent facts, fixed specifications and a general engineering principle. None of this changes with time, so we use the Present Simple.

- Form: `subject + base verb (+ -s / -es with he, she, it)`
- Negative: `subject + do / does + not + base verb`
- Question: `Do / Does + subject + base verb?`
- Signal words: always, usually, normally, every day, in general

### (b) Spoken script (practice version)

Good morning, everyone. ‖ My presentation **follows** one reinforced concrete raft foundation, from the first steel bar to the last drop of concrete, | and it **uses** nine English tenses along the way. ‖ Let's start with the facts, in the Present Simple. | Our raft **covers** four thousand five hundred square meters, | and it **is** one meter thick. | A raft foundation **distributes** heavy structural loads evenly across the soil. | Instead of many separate footings, one continuous slab **supports** every column, every retaining wall, and both stair cores. ‖ The structural drawings **define** the shape. | The red line **marks** the R.C. raft border, | the yellow squares **show** the columns, | and the hatched boxes **indicate** the lift pit and the sump pit. ‖ The site **sits** on bare, earthen ground, | and several buildings under construction **surround** it. | The team **pours** the raft at night, because cool air **slows** the setting of the concrete. ‖ These are permanent facts and fixed specifications, | so the Present Simple **is** the natural choice.

### (c) On-slide text

**One Raft, One Continuous Slab**

- 4,500 m² reinforced concrete raft, 1.0 m thick
- One slab carries every column, retaining wall and stair core
- Layout from the structural CAD: raft border, column grid, walls, stair starters, pits

> A raft foundation **distributes** heavy structural loads evenly across the soil.

### (d) Pacing and voice cues

- **Mood:** Warm, confident opening. Smile in the voice.
- **Speed:** 0.95×
- **Stress:** four thousand five hundred · one meter · evenly · permanent facts
- **Tip:** Pause fully after "Good morning, everyone." Slow down on the numbers so the audience can see them on the HUD.
- **On screen while you speak:** Elevated three-quarter aerial view (about 40°) of a 4,500 m² raft footprint on a lean-concrete blinding slab, a glowing CAD layout overlay (red raft border, cyan grid, yellow columns, magenta retaining walls, green stair starters, hatched pits), bare earthen site, access roads, and concrete-frame buildings under construction around it. Late-afternoon light.

## Slide 2: Bottom Mesh: Laid and Inspected

**Tense focus:** Past Simple + Past Continuous · **Stage:** Bottom reinforcement mesh · **Speaking time:** ≈ 69 s (131 words)

### (a) Tense explanation

**Past Simple.** Finished actions in a clear order: the crew laid, spread, placed and tied.

- Form: `subject + verb-ed (regular) or the past form (irregular: laid, spread, built)`
- Negative: `subject + did not + base verb`
- Question: `Did + subject + base verb?`
- Signal words: yesterday, last week, ago, first, then, after that, finally

**Past Continuous.** Background actions in progress at that time: the engineers were walking and checking while the crew worked. A short Past Simple event (noticed) interrupts the longer Past Continuous action.

- Form: `subject + was / were + verb-ing`
- Negative: `subject + was / were + not + verb-ing`
- Question: `Was / Were + subject + verb-ing?`
- Signal words: while, when, at that moment, at 10 o'clock yesterday, all morning

### (b) Spoken script (practice version)

Now let's go back to the first week on site. ‖ For finished actions, I'll use the Past Simple, | and for actions in progress, the Past Continuous. ‖ First, the crew **laid** the bottom reinforcement mesh across the blinding concrete. | They **spread** the long bars in one direction, | **placed** the cross bars on top, | and **tied** every crossing with wire. | They also **placed** concrete cover blocks under the mesh. ‖ While the workers **were spreading** the bars, | two engineers **were walking** the grid with the drawings. | They **were checking** the bar spacing, row by row. | While they **were checking** the third row, an engineer **noticed** a wide gap, | and the crew **fixed** it immediately. ‖ So, the Past Simple tells us what happened, | and the Past Continuous describes what **was going on** at that moment.

### (c) On-slide text

**Bottom Mesh: Laid and Inspected**

- Bottom bars spread in both directions, tied at every crossing
- Cover blocks under the mesh keep 75 mm of concrete cover
- Engineers checked the spacing row by row

> The crew **laid** the bottom reinforcement mesh and **placed** concrete cover blocks.
>
> While the workers **were spreading** the bars, two engineers **were walking** the grid.

### (d) Pacing and voice cues

- **Mood:** Storytelling, calm and clear.
- **Speed:** 0.96×
- **Stress:** laid · spread · tied · were walking · noticed
- **Tip:** Make "While the workers were spreading" sound like background (slightly softer), then lift the voice on "noticed".
- **On screen while you speak:** Zoom into the south-west zone: yellow-helmet workers carrying and spreading long bottom bars in both directions and tying crossings, small grey cover blocks under the mesh, two white-helmet engineers walking the grid with tablets.

- Negative: `subject + did not + base verb`
- Question: `Did + subject + base verb?`

Next **came** the top mesh. ‖ This **was** a clear sequence of finished steps, | so the Past Simple is the perfect tense. ‖ First, the tower crane **lifted** bundles of bars onto the chairs. | Then the crew **spread** the side bars across the full width of the raft, sixty meters. | After that, they **laid** the front bars along the whole length, eighty meters from end to end. | Finally, they **tied** every crossing | and **checked** the top cover. ‖ Listen to the verbs: | lifted and checked are regular verbs that simply add E-D, | but came, spread, and laid are irregular, | so we just have to learn them.

## Slide 5: Starter Bars: Columns, Walls, Stairs, Shaft

**Tense focus:** Past Perfect Simple + Past Perfect Continuous · **Stage:** Column, wall, stair and shaft starter bars · **Speaking time:** ≈ 79 s (147 words)

### (a) Tense explanation

**Past Perfect Simple.** Something was complete before another past moment: the meshes were finished before the starter bars arrived.

- Form: `subject + had + past participle`
- Negative: `subject + had not (hadn't) + past participle`
- Question: `Had + subject + past participle?`
- Signal words: before, after, by the time, already, until, when

**Past Perfect Continuous.** We stress how long an activity continued before that past moment: weeks of steel fixing.

- Form: `subject + had been + verb-ing`
- Negative: `subject + had not been + verb-ing`
- Question: `Had + subject + been + verb-ing?`
- Signal words: for three weeks, since, all day, how long, before

### (b) Spoken script (practice version)

Now, imagine the day the starter bars arrived. ‖ To talk about the time before that day, we need the Past Perfect. ‖ By the time the starter bars arrived, the crew **had already finished** both meshes, | and the engineers **had approved** every chair. | The workers lowered the vertical bars into the columns, | along the retaining walls, | and around the stair and shaft openings, exactly where the drawings showed them. ‖ They **had been fixing** steel for almost three weeks, | and they **had been working** long shifts every single day. ‖ Here's the difference: | "had finished" focuses on the completed result, | while "had been fixing" focuses on the long effort. ‖ And on the night of the pour, we could say: | the engineers **had inspected** the braced timber formwork before the first mixer truck arrived, | and crews **had been preparing** the site and reinforcing zones for days before the night pour began.

### (c) On-slide text

**Starter Bars: Columns, Walls, Stairs, Shaft**

- Vertical starters lowered at every column, retaining wall, stair and shaft
- Lap length left above the raft for the next stage
- Positions taken directly from the CAD layout

> The engineers **had inspected** the braced timber formwork before the first mixer truck **arrived**.
>
> Crews **had been preparing** the site and reinforcing zones for days before the night pour **began**.

### (d) Pacing and voice cues

- **Mood:** Reflective, slightly slower. Clear contrast between the two forms.
- **Speed:** 0.93×
- **Stress:** had already finished · had been fixing · result · effort
- **Tip:** Stress "had" and "been" clearly: "had BEEN fixing". Many speakers swallow them.
- **On screen while you speak:** Zoom onto the east zone: vertical starter bars lowering into column positions, a double row along the retaining wall, and bars around the stair starter and lift shaft, with engineers checking against drawings.

**ElevenLabs-ready text**


**Tense focus:** Present Continuous · **Stage:** Live night concrete pouring · **Speaking time:** ≈ 66 s (129 words)

### (a) Tense explanation

**Present Continuous.** We describe the action as it happens, at the moment of speaking, as if the audience is watching it live.

- Form: `subject + am / is / are + verb-ing`
- Negative: `subject + am / is / are + not + verb-ing`
- Question: `Am / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?`

## Grammar reference for the professor

Each tense below is listed in the course's order (1 to 9) with its form, use, the reason it appears in this presentation, the required context example, and sentences quoted from the spoken script. A printable web version with timelines is in `grammar.html`.

### 1. Present Simple

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + | subject + base verb (+ -s / -es with he, she, it) | The raft covers 4,500 m². |
| − | subject + do / does + not + base verb | The raft does not rest on piles. |
| ? | Do / Does + subject + base verb? | Does the raft carry the walls? |

**Use:** Permanent facts and fixed project specifications; General engineering principles and scientific truths; Routines and repeated actions.

**Why it is used here (slide 1):** Slide 1 describes the permanent design of the raft (its area, thickness and layout) and the engineering principle behind it. These facts are always true, so the Present Simple fits.

**Context example:** A raft foundation **distributes** heavy structural loads evenly across the soil.

**In the script:**

- *Slide 1:* My presentation **follows** one reinforced concrete raft foundation, from the first steel bar to the last drop of concrete, and it **uses** nine English tenses along the way.
- *Slide 1:* Our raft **covers** four thousand five hundred square meters, and it **is** one meter thick.
- *Slide 1:* A raft foundation **distributes** heavy structural loads evenly across the soil.
- *Slide 1:* Instead of many separate footings, one continuous slab **supports** every column, every retaining wall, and both stair cores.
- *Slide 1:* The structural drawings **define** the shape.

**Signal words:** always, usually, normally, every day, in general

**Common mistakes:**

- ~~The raft distribute the load.~~ → The raft distributes the load. (Add -s with he / she / it.)
- ~~The raft is cover 4,500 m².~~ → The raft covers 4,500 m². (Do not mix "be" with a main verb.)

### 2. Present Continuous

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + | subject + am / is / are + verb-ing | The pumps are pushing concrete. |
| − | subject + am / is / are + not + verb-ing | The trucks are not waiting long. |
| ? | Am / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing? | Is the concrete spreading evenly? |

**Use:** Actions happening now, at the moment of speaking; Temporary actions in progress around now; Live description, as if the audience is watching.

**Why it is used here (slide 8):** Slide 8 narrates the live night pour as if the audience is standing on site and watching it happen right now.

**Context example:** Workers **are guiding** the pump hoses and vibrating fresh concrete under the floodlights.

**In the script:**

- *Slide 8:* Look at the screen: this **is happening** right now, so we need the Present Continuous.
- *Slide 8:* The pumps **are pushing** concrete through their booms, and the concrete **is spreading** across the raft in layers.
- *Slide 8:* At the edge of the raft, two mixer trucks **are discharging** into the pump hoppers, more trucks **are waiting** in line, and an empty truck **is leaving** for the next load.
- *Slide 8:* Workers **are guiding** the pump hoses and vibrating fresh concrete under the floodlights.
- *Slide 8:* On the scaffolding, two workers **are moving** a hose along the edge.

**Signal words:** now, right now, at the moment, look!, listen!, tonight

**Common mistakes:**

- ~~Look! The pump pumps concrete now.~~ → Look! The pump is pumping concrete now. (Use the continuous form for actions in progress now.)
- ~~The raft is needing more concrete.~~ → The raft needs more concrete. (Stative verbs (need, know, own) usually stay simple.)

### 3. Past Simple

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + | subject + verb-ed (regular) or the past form (irregular: laid, spread, built) | The crew laid the bottom mesh. |
| − | subject + did not + base verb | The crew did not skip any crossings. |
| ? | Did + subject + base verb? | Did the engineers check the spacing? |

**Use:** Completed actions at a specific time in the past; A sequence of finished events (first, then, after that, finally).

**Why it is used here (slides 2, 4, 6):** Slides 2, 4 and 6 tell the reinforcement and formwork story as a sequence of finished steps.

**Context example:** The crew **laid** the bottom reinforcement mesh and **placed** concrete cover blocks.

**In the script:**

- *Slide 2:* First, the crew **laid** the bottom reinforcement mesh across the blinding concrete.
- *Slide 2:* They **spread** the long bars in one direction, **placed** the cross bars on top, and **tied** every crossing with wire.
- *Slide 2:* They also **placed** concrete cover blocks under the mesh.
- *Slide 2:* While they were checking the third row, an engineer **noticed** a wide gap, and the crew **fixed** it immediately.
- *Slide 4:* Next **came** the top mesh.

**Signal words:** yesterday, last week, ago, first, then, after that, finally

**Common mistakes:**

- ~~Did they laid the mesh?~~ → Did they lay the mesh? (After "did", use the base verb.)
- ~~The crew spreaded the bars.~~ → The crew spread the bars. ("Spread" is irregular: spread / spread / spread.)

### 4. Past Continuous

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + | subject + was / were + verb-ing | The engineers were checking the spacing. |
| − | subject + was / were + not + verb-ing | The crane was not working at 10:00. |
| ? | Was / Were + subject + verb-ing? | Were the workers tying the chairs? |

**Use:** An action in progress at a specific moment in the past; The background to a shorter past action (when / while); Two or more past actions happening at the same time.

**Why it is used here (slides 2, 3, 6):** Slides 2, 3 and 6 freeze busy moments on site: many crews working at once while the engineers inspected.

**Context example:** Engineers **were inspecting** the chair spacing while workers **arranged** the grid.

**In the script:**

- *Slide 2:* While the workers **were spreading** the bars, two engineers **were walking** the grid with the drawings.
- *Slide 2:* They **were checking** the bar spacing, row by row.
- *Slide 2:* While they **were checking** the third row, an engineer noticed a wide gap, and the crew fixed it immediately.
- *Slide 2:* So, the Past Simple tells us what happened, and the Past Continuous describes what **was going on** at that moment.
- *Slide 3:* What **was happening** on the raft?

**Signal words:** while, when, at that moment, at 10 o'clock yesterday, all morning

**Common mistakes:**

- ~~While they checked, the engineer was noticed a gap.~~ → While they were checking, the engineer noticed a gap. (The long background action is continuous; the short event is simple.)
- ~~The workers was tying the chairs.~~ → The workers were tying the chairs. (Plural subject: were.)

### 5. Past Perfect Simple

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + | subject + had + past participle | The crew had finished both meshes. |
| − | subject + had not (hadn't) + past participle | The trucks had not arrived yet. |
| ? | Had + subject + past participle? | Had the engineers approved the chairs? |

**Use:** An action completed before another past action or time; The "earlier past" in a story told in the past.

**Why it is used here (slide 5):** Slide 5 looks back from the day the starter bars arrived: both meshes were already complete before that moment.

**Context example:** The engineers **had inspected** the braced timber formwork before the first mixer truck **arrived**.

**In the script:**

- *Slide 5:* By the time the starter bars arrived, the crew **had already finished** both meshes, and the engineers **had approved** every chair.
- *Slide 5:* And on the night of the pour, we could say: the engineers **had inspected** the braced timber formwork before the first mixer truck arrived, and crews had been preparing the site and reinforcing zones for days before the night pour began.

**Signal words:** before, after, by the time, already, until, when

**Common mistakes:**

- ~~The crew had took the bars.~~ → The crew had taken the bars. (Use the past participle after "had".)
- ~~By the time the bars arrived, the crew finished the mesh.~~ → By the time the bars arrived, the crew had finished the mesh. ("By the time" signals the earlier past.)

### 6. Past Perfect Continuous

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + | subject + had been + verb-ing | They had been fixing steel for three weeks. |
| − | subject + had not been + verb-ing | They had not been resting. |
| ? | Had + subject + been + verb-ing? | How long had they been working? |

**Use:** The duration of an activity continuing up to a point in the past; Explaining a past situation by the long effort before it.

**Why it is used here (slide 5):** Slide 5 stresses the weeks of continuous steel fixing and preparation that led up to the pour.

**Context example:** Crews **had been preparing** the site and reinforcing zones for days before the night pour **began**.

**In the script:**

- *Slide 5:* They **had been fixing** steel for almost three weeks, and they **had been working** long shifts every single day.
- *Slide 5:* And on the night of the pour, we could say: the engineers had inspected the braced timber formwork before the first mixer truck arrived, and crews **had been preparing** the site and reinforcing zones for days before the night pour began.

**Signal words:** for three weeks, since, all day, how long, before

**Common mistakes:**

- ~~They had been fix steel.~~ → They had been fixing steel. (After "had been", use verb-ing.)
- ~~They had been knowing the plan.~~ → They had known the plan. (Stative verbs avoid the continuous form.)

### 7. Present Perfect Simple

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| + | subject + have / has + past participle | We have coordinated 75 trucks. |
| − | subject + have / has + not + past participle | We haven't forgotten anything. |
| ? | Have / Has + subject + past participle? | Have the engineers signed the checklist? |

**Use:** Completed actions with a result or relevance now; Totals, counts and achievements up to now; Unfinished time periods (today, tonight, so far) and no exact past time.

**Why it is used here (slide 7):** Slide 7 reports everything that is already mobilized. The actions are finished, but the result (a site ready to pour) matters now.

**Context example:** We **have coordinated** 6 batching plants, 75 mixer trucks, and 11 concrete pumps for tonight's pour.

**In the script:**

**Signal words:** already, just, yet, so far, up to now, ever, never

**Common mistakes:**

- ~~We have coordinated the trucks yesterday.~~ → We coordinated the trucks yesterday. (A finished time (yesterday) needs the Past Simple.)
- ~~The engineers has signed.~~ → The engineers have signed. (Plural subject: have.)
